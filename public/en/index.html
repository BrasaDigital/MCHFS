About the programme

Despite countless efforts, programmes and innovations in maternal and child health, the prevalence of preventable maternal and neonatal mortality remains high worldwide. Part of the problem is unequal access to health services for women and adolescents, low adherence to prenatal consultations, and a mismatch between what health services offer and what women and adolescents actually need — compounded by local cultural beliefs that directly affect neonatal morbidity and mortality.

A Humanitarian Aid through a Global Mentoring Programme

The Maternal and Child Health Friendly Standards online Mentoring Programme was designed to refresh, transform and strengthen the on-the-ground performance of frontline professionals in public or private health facilities with maternity wards, filling the gaps in essential, quality care for pregnant women, newborns and mothers. The 15-month programme is led by Ms Regina Pereira da Silva, an international/global health consultant who has trained more than 4,000 frontline professionals in maternal and child health and nutrition since the 1990s, and Ms Vanessa Barroso, who holds a Master's in Public Health from the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine.

The programme is completely free of charge and does not interfere with professionals' duties at their health facility. It is recommended that up to three frontline professionals working with pregnant women, labour and birth, and immediate postpartum from the same health facility with a maternity ward attend together. Throughout the programme, up-to-date resources are shared for every module's subject, and the mentors facilitate online training aligned with the Baby-Friendly Standards, version 2020. Two free certificates are issued on completion.

We are tackling the problem at its root, mentoring frontline professionals to transform their performance where essential antenatal care is the cornerstone of the Mentoring Programme — an innovative approach to prevent not only maternal morbidity and mortality, but especially prematurity and low birth weight. Beyond mentoring labour, childbirth and postpartum care, our main effort is to transform assistance before conception, following the Ten Golden Rules for Future Parents, with Prenatal Education as a pillar for change. As part of building the Antenatal Well Care Package, midwives in the programme are designing action plans based on the Ten Golden Rules for Future Parents — including pre-conception clinics at their own workplace — and rethinking antenatal care beyond clinical appointments alone, to embrace every woman and adolescent who needs care. We are already mentoring frontline midwives in Burundi, Cameroon, Ghana, Pakistan, Tunisia, Uganda and Zimbabwe.
